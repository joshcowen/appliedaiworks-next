# Process Automation for HVAC Companies: What Actually Works

The HVAC automations that actually pay off in a real operation are: automated job follow-up texts after service calls, AI-drafted maintenance agreement renewal emails, dispatch-to-invoice handoffs that don't require your office staff to retype anything, and missed-call text-back sequences that keep leads from going to your competitor. These are not big IT projects. Most of them run inside tools you may already be paying for, like ServiceTitan, Jobber, or a simple Make.com workflow connected to a phone system like CallRail. Setup time ranges from two hours to a full day per automation. The payoff is measurable within 30 days.

## Why Most HVAC Automation Advice Is Useless

Generic content tells you to "automate your workflows" and then shows you a screenshot from a SaaS demo that looks nothing like your actual operation. That is not helpful when you are running 12 trucks, one dispatcher named Karen, and a QuickBooks file that has its own personality.

The automations that work for HVAC companies are specific to how HVAC actually runs: seasonal volume swings, same-day emergency calls, equipment serial numbers that need to follow a job from estimate to install to warranty, and customers who expect a human on the phone but also won't call back if you miss them. Any automation you build has to work inside those constraints or it creates more problems than it solves.

## The Four Automations Worth Building First

### 1. Missed-Call Text-Back

If your shop misses a call during the lunch hour or after 5 PM, that lead is usually gone in under three minutes. They called the next company. A missed-call text-back sends an automatic reply the moment a call goes unanswered: something like "Hey, this is [Company]. We just missed you. What's going on with your system? We'll call you right back." You set it up once inside CallRail or your phone system, or through a Make.com automation tied to your CRM. It takes about two hours.

In the field, HVAC companies running this report converting leads they would have lost entirely, especially on nights and weekends when no one is staffed to answer. One way to think about it: if your average HVAC job is worth $400 and you are missing four calls a week after hours, recovering even two of those is $800 in revenue per week from a two-hour setup. The math is not complicated.

**How to set it up step by step:**

1. Log into CallRail (or your phone system). Look for a "missed call" trigger or notification setting.
2. Connect that trigger to a text message action. Most phone systems have this built in. If yours does not, a Make.com workflow watching for missed call webhooks from your phone system will do the same thing.
3. Write your text. Keep it under 160 characters. Include your company name, acknowledge the missed call, and ask one open question about their system. Do not paste in a menu of options.
4. Test it by calling your own business line from a cell phone and letting it go to voicemail. Confirm the text arrives within 60 seconds.
5. Check your CRM the next morning for any responses. Route them to whoever handles inbound scheduling.

That is the whole setup. If you hit a snag at the Make.com step, their template library has a "missed call SMS" workflow you can clone and modify in about 30 minutes.

### 2. Post-Service Follow-Up Sequence

After a technician closes a job, two things should happen automatically: a review request goes out within an hour, and a maintenance agreement offer follows three days later if the customer doesn't already have one. Most field service platforms like Jobber or ServiceTitan can trigger these natively. If yours can't, a Make.com workflow watching your job status field will do it.

The key is timing. A review request sent an hour after a good service call converts at a much higher rate than one sent a week later when the customer has already moved on. For context, Google review conversion on same-day requests typically runs 15 to 25 percent for service businesses. Wait a week and that drops to under five percent. The window is real and it closes fast.

The maintenance agreement follow-up three days out works because the customer still remembers the tech, the problem is still fresh in their mind, and they have not yet filed the invoice away mentally as a closed chapter. Keep that second message short: one sentence about what was serviced, one sentence about what a maintenance agreement covers, one link to schedule or call.

### 3. Maintenance Agreement Renewal Emails

This is where AI actually helps, not just automation. Pull your expiring agreements into a list, run that list through a ChatGPT prompt that references the customer's equipment type and last service date, and generate a personalized renewal email for each one. Your office staff reviews and sends. What used to take a Saturday morning of writing now takes 20 minutes.

The emails read like a person wrote them because a person reviewed them. Josh Cowen at Applied AI Works calls this "AI as a first draft machine," and for renewal campaigns it is the clearest ROI of any AI application in the trades.

**A starter prompt you can use:**

> "You are a friendly HVAC service coordinator. Write a renewal email for a customer named [First Name] whose maintenance agreement expires on [Date]. Their system is a [Equipment Brand and Type] installed in [Year]. Their last service was [Service Summary]. The tone should be warm and direct, like a local company that knows them. Do not use bullet points. Keep it under 150 words. End with a clear call to action to call or click to renew."

Paste that into ChatGPT with the real customer data filled in, review the output, adjust anything that doesn't sound right for your company's voice, and send. Run 20 renewals in the time it used to take to write three.

**On timing:** Send renewal emails 45 days before expiration, not 30. Customers who wait until the last minute are harder to close and more likely to price-shop. At 45 days you get the ones who are simply ready to renew without friction.

### 4. Dispatch-to-Invoice Handoff

The single biggest time waste in most HVAC offices is someone retyping information that already exists somewhere else. The tech writes job notes in the field. Someone in the office reads those notes and types them into the invoice. That is double work, and it introduces errors.

If you are on ServiceTitan, the native workflow tools handle most of this. If you are on a lighter platform, an Otter.ai transcription of the tech's voice notes, piped through a Make.com automation into your invoicing system, gets you most of the way there. It is not perfect on day one. You spend a week cleaning up edge cases. Then it runs.

**What the handoff flow looks like in practice:**

- Tech finishes the job and dictates a voice note into Otter.ai on their phone: equipment model, work performed, parts used, anything the customer mentioned about future needs.
- Otter transcribes it automatically and sends the text to a shared folder or directly to a Make.com webhook.
- Make.com parses the note and pre-fills the invoice fields in your billing system with the job details.
- Office staff opens a nearly complete invoice, confirms the numbers, and sends it.

The first week you will find gaps: parts your tech abbreviates differently than your system expects, customer names that don't match exactly, occasional Otter transcription errors on technical terms. Build a short glossary of your most common part names and equipment abbreviations, feed that into your Make.com parsing step, and most of those gaps close. By week two it is mostly hands-off.

## Seasonal Follow-Up Sequences: The Automation Most HVAC Companies Skip

Most HVAC companies have a busy season and a slow season. They know this. They do not act on it systematically because building a seasonal outreach campaign feels like a marketing project and nobody has time for that in July when the phones are ringing.

Here is a simpler way to think about it. You have two campaigns to build once and schedule to repeat every year.

**Spring campaign (send in late March):** Target every customer who had a heating service call between October and February. The message is simple: summer is coming, now is the time to check the cooling side before it's 90 degrees and everyone needs a tech at once. Offer a specific date range for tune-up appointments. Make.com or your CRM can pull that customer list automatically based on job type and date range.

**Fall campaign (send in early September):** Target every customer who had a cooling service call between May and August. Mirror the spring message, but for heating. One additional angle for fall: maintenance agreement upsell. Customers who just had a summer cooling repair are warmer to a maintenance agreement conversation than cold-list customers.

Both campaigns take about three hours to build the first time. After that, you schedule them to run automatically on the same dates each year. You review the list in the week before it sends, pull out anyone who has moved or is no longer a customer, and let it go. This is the kind of automation that compounds quietly in the background while you are focused on running jobs.

## What to Skip (For Now)

AI-powered scheduling optimization sounds great until you remember that your best tech only goes to certain parts of town and your dispatcher knows which customers need extra time. Don't automate dispatch routing until you have documented those rules somewhere a system can actually read. Same goes for chatbots on your website. A bad chatbot that can't answer "do you service Carrier units" is worse than no chatbot at all. Build the simple stuff first, prove it works, then layer in complexity.

## Real Setup Costs and Time

Missed-call text-back: two hours, often free inside your existing phone system or $20/month through Make.com.

Post-service follow-up: two to four hours if your CRM supports triggers natively, one full day if you are building it in Make.com from scratch.

AI renewal emails: three hours to build the prompt and test it, then 20 minutes per campaign to run it. ChatGPT Plus is $20/month.

Dispatch-to-invoice handoff: one day to build, one week to stabilize. Otter.ai Business runs about $20/user/month.

Seasonal follow-up sequences: three hours to build the first time, then automated every year after that. Cost is whatever your email platform charges to send, usually negligible.

None of this requires a developer. It requires someone willing to sit down and figure it out, or a partner who has already done it for companies like yours.

## How to Know If You Are Ready

You are ready to build automations if you can answer yes to two questions. First: do you have a process that runs the same way every time, or close to it? Second: is someone on your team doing the same repetitive task at least three times a week? If yes to both, that process is automatable. If your process is chaotic and different every time, automate nothing yet. Fix the process first. AI and automation are multipliers. They make a good process faster and a bad process worse.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the easiest automation to set up for an HVAC company?

Missed-call text-back is the easiest and fastest. It takes about two hours to configure inside most phone systems or through a Make.com workflow. When a call goes unanswered, the system texts the caller automatically. For HVAC companies, this recovers leads that would otherwise go to a competitor, especially after hours and on weekends when no one is staffed to answer.

### Does process automation work with ServiceTitan or Jobber?

Yes. Both platforms have native automation triggers for things like job status changes, follow-up messages, and review requests. ServiceTitan has more built-in options. Jobber covers the basics. For anything either platform can't handle natively, Make.com connects to both through API or webhook and lets you build custom automations without writing code.

### How much does it cost to automate an HVAC business?

The tools themselves are cheap. Make.com starts around $20 per month. ChatGPT Plus is $20 per month. Otter.ai Business runs about $20 per user per month. Your real cost is setup time, which ranges from two hours for a simple text-back to one full day for a dispatch-to-invoice handoff. Most HVAC companies see measurable return within 30 days of a working automation.

### Can AI replace my HVAC dispatcher?

No, and you should not try. Dispatch involves judgment calls that depend on relationships, geography, and technician strengths that no current AI system can replicate reliably. What AI can do is handle the repetitive communication around dispatch: confirmation texts, ETA updates, post-job follow-ups. That frees your dispatcher to focus on the calls that actually need a human.

### Where do HVAC companies waste the most time that automation could fix?

The biggest time sinks are retyping information that already exists somewhere else, manually chasing maintenance agreement renewals, and following up on leads that came in after hours. According to Josh Cowen at Applied AI Works, the dispatch-to-invoice handoff and missed-call recovery are the two places HVAC companies consistently leave the most money on the table before they automate.

### How do seasonal demand swings affect HVAC process automation?

Seasonal volume is actually one of the best reasons to build automation early. When call volume spikes in June or January, your staff is already stretched thin. A missed-call text-back and a pre-scheduled seasonal tune-up campaign mean you capture more leads and send more outreach without adding headcount. You build the campaigns once, schedule them to repeat annually, and they run whether you get 20 calls that day or 80.

### What should an HVAC company automate first if they have never done it before?

Start with missed-call text-back. It takes two hours, costs almost nothing, and you will know within a week whether leads are responding. Once that is running and you trust it, add a post-service review request triggered an hour after job close. Those two automations alone cover the biggest revenue leaks most HVAC companies have before they ever touch anything more complex.
